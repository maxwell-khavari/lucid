Measured result

On held-out episodes with stirs stronger than anything in training, the network alone drifts to 24.6% error against the solver. With the self-check gating 14% of frames to the solver, error is 6.1%. Spending the same budget on random or scheduled frames leaves about 19%. The self-check ranks its own worst frames with an AUROC of 0.99. A hand-written rule built for this exact disturbance comes close (6.7% at 20%), and below a 10% budget a physics residual check does as well or better.

These numbers are for this 2D toy setup only. Full tables and baselines

Where the pattern applies

Neural surrogates are being used in place of slow solvers in weather forecasting, engineering CFD, chip and battery thermals, digital twins and real-time graphics. They share one failure: outside their training range they stay confident and become wrong. A surrogate that estimates its own error, and defers to the trusted method when it should, is one way to make them safer to use.

Lucid demonstrates the pattern. It is not a tool for any of these fields.

Why 2D, and no speedup

A 64 × 64 2D grid trains in 2.3 hours on one laptop and runs live in a browser. At this size the reference solver is about as fast as the network, so the demo shows error control, not speed. Surrogates save time where the real method is expensive, such as 3D or high resolution. This project does not test those settings.

Prior work

Pairing a surrogate with a solver fallback is established. Lucid claims no novelty; it is an open, reproducible, runnable comparison of gating strategies.
